Avoidance means a certain loss exposure is never acquired or undertaken, or an existing loss exposure is abandoned.
An example of the first case, flood losses can be avoided by building a new plant on high ground, well above a floodplain. An example of the second case, a pharmaceutical firm that markets a drug with dangerous side effects can remove the drug from the market to avoid possible legal liability.
The main advantage of avoidance is that the chance of loss from the exposure is reduced or eliminated. There are two main disadvantages,
  1. One might not be able to avoid all losses. For example, one might not be able to avoid the premature death of a family member.
  2. Avoiding the exposure might not be feasible or practical. For example, a paint factory can avoid losses arising from the production of paint, but without paint production, the firm will not be in business.
Loss prevention refers to measures that reduce the frequency of a particular loss.
For example, measures that reduce truck accidents include driver training, zero tolerance for alcohol or drug abuse, and strict enforcement of safety rules.
Loss reduction refers to measures that reduce the severity of a loss after it occurs.
For example, installation of automatic sprinkler system for fires, and first-aid boxes in production areas.
Duplication refers to having backups or copies of important documents or property available in case a loss occurs.
For example, keeping a spare tire in the trunk, and making copies of key business records.
Separation means dividing the assets exposed to loss to minimize the harm from a single event.
For example, a manufacturing company might divide the production area of a plant into four quadrants by using thick concrete walls, so that if a fire damages production in one quadrant, the walls prevent the fire from spreading to other areas.
Diversification refers to reducing the chance of loss by spreading the loss exposure across different parties, securities, or transactions.
For example, having contracts with several suppliers can minimize the risk of relying on a single supplier.
